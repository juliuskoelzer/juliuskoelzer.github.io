<p class="ltw-standfirst">The AfD is approaching an absolute majority in Saxony-Anhalt – on sharply higher turnout. Drawing on the results from the state's 218 municipalities and fine-grained structural data, this piece analyses the geographic patterns behind that result.</p>
<p class="ltw-byline">by <strong>Julius Kölzer</strong> · 7 September 2026</p>

## 1. Turnout Rises Across the Countryside

The big game changer in this state election is the enormous rise in turnout. With almost 250,000 additional votes, it rose by 17.5 percentage points, from 60.3 to 77.8 per cent. The increase varies considerably between municipalities. In the more rural municipalities of the Harz, the Salzlandkreis and the Burgenlandkreis in the south of the state, the rise frequently exceeds 20 percentage points. In the state's two large cities, Halle and Magdeburg, it is markedly smaller: at roughly 15 percentage points each, it even falls below the statewide average.

<figure class="ltw-fig-narrow">
  <img src="/images/en/ltw26_turnout_karte.png" alt="Map: increase in turnout in percentage points by municipality">
  <figcaption>Change in turnout compared with the 2021 state election, in percentage points.</figcaption>
</figure>

Owing to their population size, the two cities do account for just under 43,000 of the roughly 250,000 newly mobilised votes of former non-voters. The much larger mobilisation effect, however, shows up in the rest of the state: in the municipalities outside the two large cities, around 205,000 votes were added by people who had not voted before. The strong rise in turnout in rural areas is also visible in the scatterplots. A larger increase in turnout tends to go together with greater rurality, measured among other things by population density and population size. Turnout also tends to rise more strongly in municipalities with greater population loss and a more peripheral location.

Beyond that, turnout rose most where the AfD had already performed above average in 2021. Conversely, the increase tends to be smaller in regions where the parties of the political centre were more firmly anchored.

  <figcaption>The six municipal characteristics most strongly associated with the rise in turnout, plus the 2021 results.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Mobilising Former Non-Voters

That the rise in turnout was driven largely by party mobilisation was already evident on election night. The AfD in particular succeeded in mobilising former non-voters on a considerable scale. With a net gain of around 157,000 votes from the ranks of previous non-voters, its mobilisation effort was more than twice as large as that of all other parties combined.

This finding is not new, however (cf. Schäfer 2024; Vehrkamp/Wegschaider 2017). That the AfD in particular manages to mobilise groups that had previously stayed away from the polls has been well documented in the literature since the 2017 federal election at the latest (cf. Hansen/Olsen 2019; Immerzeel/Pickup 2015). The mobilisability of these groups is often linked to political alienation, lower trust in established political institutions, and socio-economic and spatial inequalities (cf. Deppisch et al. 2019; Förtner et al. 2021; Schulte-Cloos/Leininger 2022). In structurally weaker regions in particular, such experiences of political and social marginalisation can become concentrated (cf. Deppisch/Klärner 2025; Schäfer 2024). In many rural parts of Saxony-Anhalt, turnout fell to barely 40 per cent during the 2000s.

### Who Benefits from the Extra Votes

The municipality-level analyses confirm the AfD's mobilisation gains. The increase in the AfD's vote share since 2021 correlates much more strongly with the rise in turnout than the changes in the other parties' vote shares. For the combined vote share of the parties of the political centre – CDU, SPD, Greens and FDP – the relationship is even negative: the more turnout rose, the larger their losses compared with the previous state election.[^1]

## 2. The AfD Dominates Rural Saxony-Anhalt

It is above all through mobilising non-voters in rural areas that the AfD became the strongest party in every municipality of the state. The political map shifted from CDU dominance in 2021 to AfD dominance in 2026. In 2021, the CDU was the strongest party in 214 of 218 municipalities; in 2026, it is the AfD in all 218. The AfD's gains are largest in the south and centre of Saxony-Anhalt – in the Burgenlandkreis, Mansfeld-Südharz, the Salzlandkreis and the Saalekreis, for instance. Its gains are markedly smaller in Halle, Magdeburg and Wernigerode. The CDU loses everywhere, most heavily in Wittenberg, Dessau-Roßlau, the Jerichower Land and the Burgenlandkreis. It holds up comparatively well in the Altmark, in the district of Stendal and in the two large cities. The SPD and The Left show a similarly more urban profile: both do relatively better in Halle, Magdeburg and parts of the north, while The Left loses most in Mansfeld-Südharz, Anhalt-Bitterfeld and the Salzlandkreis. The Greens gain mainly in Halle and Magdeburg, with much weaker gains in the south; the BSW, by contrast, shows a fairly even distribution with somewhat stronger results in Dessau-Roßlau, Stendal, the Harz and the Salzlandkreis.

### Town vs. Country

Regional differences notwithstanding, the clearest dividing line runs between the state's three larger cities and the rest of Saxony-Anhalt. In municipalities with fewer than 50,000 inhabitants, the AfD is dominant throughout, while the other parties perform considerably worse there on average. In municipalities with fewer than 5,000 inhabitants, the AfD reaches as much as 51 per cent of the vote, and even in mid-sized municipalities of 20,000 to 50,000 inhabitants it still takes 45 per cent. Only in Halle, Magdeburg and Dessau-Roßlau does its share drop markedly, to 32 per cent. At the same time, the Greens in particular, at 17 per cent, but also the SPD and The Left, perform much better there than in smaller municipalities. The CDU remains comparatively stable across the size classes and loses some ground only in the largest category. Overall, the election results of the three larger cities thus differ clearly from the rest of the state. These patterns, too, are not new. The urban-rural divide in voting behaviour is particularly pronounced in eastern Germany, not least because of the stark demographic contrast (cf. Haffert 2022; Haffert/Mitteregger 2023; Kröhnert/Vollmer 2012; Leibert 2016; Deppisch/Klärner 2025).

<figure class="ltw-fig-narrow">
  <img src="/images/en/ltw26_groessenklassen.png" alt="Stacked bar chart: vote shares by municipality size class">

The change in vote shares across the size classes also underlines the AfD's grown dominance in rural areas. The smaller the municipality, the larger its gains. The SPD and the Greens, by contrast, gain mainly in the larger and more urban municipalities. The Left's losses are also much smaller there than in smaller municipalities. This reflects the stronger urbanisation of their electorates that was already visible at the 2025 federal election – which, in a predominantly rural state like Saxony-Anhalt, meets difficult structural conditions.

## 3. What the Parties' Strongholds Look Like

The different socio-spatial profiles of the parties' strongholds also show up in a range of structural characteristics. The AfD's profile is particularly clear: its strongholds are more often smaller municipalities with more negative population change, stronger out-migration and a higher average age. At the same time, the shares of graduates, foreign nationals and women as well as purchasing power are lower there, while the share of the manufacturing sector lies above the state median. The socio-spatial profile of the municipalities with particularly high AfD results thus fits largely with the findings of previous research (cf. Höhne et al. 2025; Schütze 2023; Kröhnert/Vollmer 2012; Leibert 2016). High AfD shares are frequently concentrated in areas marked by socio-economic weakness, demographic shrinkage and further characteristics of relative deprivation of the social space (cf. Greve et al. 2023; DIW Berlin 2026; Bayerlein et al. 2026). These patterns are also consistent with earlier analyses at constituency and municipality level (cf. Franz et al. 2018; Höhne et al. 2025; Bayerlein et al. 2026; Lux 2018; Rippl/Seipel 2018; Bergmann et al. 2017; Goerres et al. 2018).

An almost opposite pattern appears for the Greens. Their strongest municipalities are larger, growing faster and have a more positive migration balance; at the same time, above-average numbers of graduates and people without German citizenship live there. The lower average age and the higher share of women are also striking, underlining the markedly more urban social profile of their strongholds. The SPD, too, is stronger in growing municipalities with a higher share of graduates and greater purchasing power, though its profile is less polarised overall. The political geography of Saxony-Anhalt thus at least partly displays the spatial patterns discussed in the literature under the heading of the "geography of discontent" (cf. Rodríguez-Pose 2018; Dijkstra et al. 2020; for Germany Greve et al. 2023; Förtner et al. 2021).

For The Left, by contrast, elements of an old and a new electorate seem to come together. On the one hand, its strongholds are more often larger municipalities with a higher share of foreign nationals, a higher share of women and a more positive migration balance – characteristics of a more urban electorate. On the other hand, weaker population change and a comparatively higher average age remain visible, pointing to the continued importance of older East German voter groups. The formative role of the post-1990 transformation experience for this generation has been described extensively in the literature (cf. Mau 2019, 2024; Mau et al. 2023; Holtmann et al. 2014). The CDU and the FDP lie closer to the state median on many structural characteristics and thus have less pronounced socio-spatial strongholds. The BSW, in turn, partly resembles the AfD's profile – for instance in municipality size, population change and average age – but much less markedly (cf. Thomeczek 2025; Heckmann et al. 2025; Höhne et al. 2025).

## 4. The Parties One by One

How these structural characteristics relate to the parties' spatial support patterns and their change is examined more closely in this final section. For the AfD, previous research – especially on eastern Germany – points to a spatial continuity with earlier successes of far-right parties. The AfD has so far been able to mobilise voters above all where electoral support for other far-right parties was already firmly anchored in the 1990s and 2000s (cf. Cantoni et al. 2019; Weisskircher 2020; Heinze et al. 2026; Kölzer et al. 2025; Schulte-Cloos 2022). Saxony-Anhalt is a particularly instructive case: as early as 1998 the DVU entered the state parliament with 12.9 per cent, and in 2011 the NPD only narrowly missed the threshold.

The current election, too, shows a clear municipality-level relationship between the earlier vote shares of far-right parties and the AfD's strength today. At the same time, that relationship has weakened noticeably over time. In 2016, the earlier results of the DVU, NPD and other far-right parties were a much stronger predictor of the AfD's performance than in 2026, as the declining correlation coefficient shows. This suggests that the AfD's spatial voter base has broadened beyond the former strongholds of organised right-wing extremism. The party now achieves high vote shares even in municipalities where far-right parties had previously been comparatively weak. The finding can thus be read as an expression of the AfD's growing electoral entrenchment and social broadening. Its support is today less confined to those milieus and places that were already characterised by a pronounced far-right voting tradition before its rise. This is consistent with findings on the changed composition of the AfD electorate and on the party's increasing electoral anchoring in eastern Germany (cf. Hansen/Olsen 2019; Arzheimer/Berning 2019; Pesthy et al. 2021; Heinze et al. 2026; Weisskircher 2020).

### AfD

<figure>
  <img src="/images/en/ltw26_afd_persistenz.png" alt="Scatterplots: far-right votes before 2016 and AfD results in 2016, 2021, 2026">
  <figcaption>AfD vote share in 2016, 2021 and 2026 against the pre-2016 far-right share, identical axes.</figcaption>
</figure>

A look at the AfD's gains also shows that its growth is no longer confined to the former strongholds of far-right parties. The increase does tend to be larger where the DVU, NPD and Republikaner already had higher vote shares before 2016[^2], but the relationship is only moderate. The AfD's gains thus by no means stem only from the former strongholds of right-wing extremism. They are much more closely related to the AfD's result at the 2021 state election. The party continues to gain most where it is already electorally anchored, yet it also grows substantially outside its previous strongholds. The strongest relationship, finally, is with the gains at the 2025 federal election. This suggests that the 2026 result continues less the old geographic patterns of right-wing extremism than the more recent spatial dynamic of the AfD's own growth.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/en/ltw26_afd_patterns.png" alt="Scatterplots: AfD surge against prior results and the 2025 federal gain">
  <figcaption>Rise in the AfD vote share since 2021 compared with prior results.</figcaption>
</figure>

Finally, the relationships sketched above between structural characteristics and the parties' electoral support are brought together in joint regression models. For each party, two separate standardised OLS models are estimated: the first model looks at the 2026 vote share, the second at the change compared with the 2021 state election. The coefficients describe the relationship between a structural characteristic and the election result while simultaneously accounting for the other variables in the model.[^3]

A methodological note before turning to the results: all analyses in this piece are based on aggregate data. Election results and structural characteristics are available for Saxony-Anhalt's 218 municipalities, not for individual voters. Relationships at this level therefore describe in which municipalities parties are strong or gain – not which people vote for them. If the AfD's share is higher in municipalities with high unemployment, it does not follow that unemployed people are more likely to vote AfD. That inference from the municipality to the individual would be the classic ecological fallacy (cf. Robinson 1950; King 1997; Lengfeld 2017; Lux 2018; Rippl/Seipel 2018).

For the AfD, the model for the 2026 vote share continues to show a positive relationship with earlier support for far-right parties. Higher unemployment also goes together with higher AfD shares, while larger municipalities and higher purchasing power are associated with lower results. This pattern matches known findings on the party's particular strength in rural areas and in regions with greater labour-market problems. For the gains since 2021, however, the picture shifts. The AfD's gains show much less clear relationships with the spatial and structural characteristics included, which is likely also because the party gained strongly almost everywhere. Significantly larger gains are found above all in smaller municipalities and where the share of women and purchasing power are lower. The earlier strength of far-right parties also remains positively related to the gains, though the relationship is now only weakly significant. A municipality's share of foreign nationals, by contrast, is significantly associated neither with general AfD support nor with its gains.

</figure>

### CDU

The CDU shows a clearly different pattern from the AfD. For the 2026 vote share there are several statistically significant relationships: higher CDU shares are found above all in municipalities with a larger share of older residents, larger households and a stronger manufacturing sector, although these relationships are rather weak. For the change since 2021, the structural pattern is much weaker still. The only statistically significant factor is the share of residents aged over 65: in older municipalities the CDU's losses are smaller. For the remaining characteristics there are no statistically significant relationships.

  <img src="/images/en/ltw26_koef_cdu.png" alt="Coefficient plot: determinants of the CDU result">
  <figcaption>CDU – 2026 vote share and change since 2021.</figcaption>
</figure>

### SPD

For the SPD, the 2026 vote share shows a comparatively clear pattern concentrated on a few characteristics. Statistically significant, first, is the negative relationship with the share of the manufacturing sector: in more industrial districts the SPD's vote share tends to be lower. The relationship with the earlier strength of far-right parties is even clearer: where the DVU, NPD and Republikaner had higher vote shares before 2016, the SPD is markedly weaker today. For the remaining structural characteristics, the joint model shows no statistically significant relationships with the 2026 vote share. For the change since 2021 the pattern is weaker still: none of the relationships included is statistically significant, which fits with the SPD holding its vote shares comparatively stable across different types of municipality.

### The Left

For The Left, the relationships are less clear-cut overall, which may also reflect the composition of its current electorate. On the one hand, the party remains anchored in parts of its older, more distinctly East German voter base; on the other, it increasingly gains in larger and more urban municipalities. Such different social and spatial subgroups can cause individual relationships to partly cancel each other out in the joint regression model. Accordingly, only a few structural characteristics show a clear relationship with the 2026 vote share. Statistically significant and positive is above all the share of foreign nationals, while the earlier strength of far-right parties is negatively related to The Left's result today. For the change since 2021, municipality size stands out in particular: in larger municipalities the party's losses are significantly smaller. This suggests that The Left is better able to hold its ground in more urban areas.

### Greens

For the Greens, the socio-spatial differences are especially pronounced. For the 2026 vote share, statistically significantly higher values are found in larger municipalities, with a higher share of women and a higher share of graduates. At the same time, the Greens do worse where the share of residents aged over 65, unemployment and the share of the manufacturing sector are higher. The joint model thus confirms the markedly more urban and socially specific profile of their electorate seen earlier. The change since 2021 shows a similar picture: larger gains are found above all in larger municipalities and where the share of women is higher and the share of older residents lower. A stronger manufacturing sector also goes together with smaller gains, while the remaining characteristics in the model show no statistically significant relationships with the change.

  <img src="/images/en/ltw26_koef_gruene.png" alt="Coefficient plot: determinants of the Greens' result">
  <figcaption>Greens – 2026 vote share and change since 2021.</figcaption>

### BSW

Finally, the BSW shows a comparatively weak socio-spatial profile. Since the party is standing in a Saxony-Anhalt state election for the first time, only the 2026 vote share can be modelled. Statistically significant, first, is the negative relationship with population change: in municipalities with stronger population decline, the BSW tends to achieve higher vote shares. At the same time, its support is lower where far-right parties were already particularly strong before 2016; this relationship, too, is statistically significant. For municipality size, age, the shares of women and foreign nationals, as well as unemployment, purchasing power, the manufacturing sector and the share of graduates, there are no statistically significant relationships. Support for the BSW is thus much harder to locate along individual socio-spatial characteristics than that for the AfD or the Greens.
