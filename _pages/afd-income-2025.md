<p class="ltw-standfirst">

Support for the AfD is often linked to poverty and low incomes. Survey data from the 2025 federal election paint a different picture: AfD voting varies only weakly with household income. Formal education matters far more.

</p>

<p class="ltw-byline">

by <strong><a href="https://juliuskoelzer.de/">Julius Kölzer</a></strong> · October 2026

</p>

In public debate, support for the AfD is repeatedly associated with “being poor” and with low incomes. A look at survey data, however, suggests that income is a rather weak socio-demographic characteristic for explaining who votes AfD.

The analysis uses the post-election cross-section of the German Longitudinal Election Study (GLES) for the 2025 federal election. Besides their vote, respondents were asked about the monthly net income of their household. The figures below are based on all respondents who report voting for a party with their second vote and who state their household income (n = 5,321).

## 1. AfD support varies only weakly with income

Looking at the AfD's share of the second vote by income group, the picture is clear: overall, AfD voting varies only weakly along the income distribution. Moreover, AfD support is not highest in the lowest income groups. Among respondents with a monthly household net income below €1,500, the AfD's share is 17 percent – about as high as among respondents with middle and higher incomes, who have several thousand euros more at their disposal each month. The highest share, 18 percent, is found among households with €2,000 to €2,500. Only above a monthly household net income of €5,000 does AfD support drop noticeably, to 10 and finally 7 percent.

The same pattern appears when AfD voting is looked at separately for East and West Germany. In the East, AfD support is considerably higher across all income groups, at 22 to 29 percent up to an income of €5,000. In the West, the line is almost flat at 13 to 15 percent. In both parts of the country, support only falls in the two highest income groups.

## 2. Controlling for age, education and region weakens the link further

A simple breakdown by income says little about whether income itself is related to AfD voting. People with low incomes differ in other respects too, for instance in age, household size, educational attainment or whether they live in East or West Germany. Once age, sex, education, household size and East/West are taken into account in a regression model, the relationship between income and AfD voting becomes even weaker. The predicted AfD share then ranges between 18 and 10 percent; the drop at the top of the income distribution is much smaller than without controls.

Put differently: comparing respondents with low incomes (here below €2,000 household net income) directly with other income groups, a significantly higher probability of voting AfD is found only in comparison with respondents with very high incomes. Compared with the next income group, the median group or the group above the mean income, the differences are small and not statistically significant. This holds with and without controls; with controls, the gap to the highest income group shrinks from 11 to around 7 percentage points.

## 3. The AfD electorate is concentrated in the lower and middle middle class

Compared with other parties, too, the AfD does not do substantially better among voters in low income groups. As noted, its share is very stable across most income groups and only falls at very high incomes. Votes for the CDU/CSU and the Greens vary much more strongly with income: both are clearly weaker among low earners and considerably stronger in the top income groups.

Reversing the perspective and looking at the income distribution within each party's electorate shows that AfD voters are concentrated in the lower to middle middle class. Around 47 percent of AfD voters live in households with less than €3,000 net income per month, while 15 percent live in households with €5,000 or more. In terms of income structure, the AfD electorate thus closely resembles those of the SPD, the BSW and the Left. It does not, however, have as distinctive an income profile as the electorates of the Greens and the FDP, of whom 37 and 38 percent respectively have at least €5,000 a month at their disposal.

## 4. The Left is much more a party of low incomes

Compared with the AfD, it is above all the vote for the Left that varies more strongly with income. Unlike for the AfD, its support is highest among respondents with the lowest incomes, even after controlling for the other characteristics, and then declines quite steadily: from 16 percent below €1,500 to 4 percent in the highest income group.

Accordingly, respondents with low incomes are significantly more likely to vote for the Left already compared with middle and slightly higher incomes – and not only compared with respondents with the highest incomes. The CDU/CSU shows the reverse pattern: low-income respondents are around 6 to 8 percentage points less likely to vote for it than the median and mean groups, and 19 points less likely than the highest income group. For the AfD, by contrast, only the comparison with the highest income group is significant.

## 5. Education explains more than income

As already indicated, formal education plays a much larger role for AfD voting than income. When both characteristics are estimated jointly in one model, the difference in predicted AfD support between education groups is far greater than between income groups: among respondents with a lower secondary school qualification (Hauptschule) it is 24 percent, among those with the Abitur 5 percent. Support also falls much more evenly as education rises. Along the income distribution, by contrast, it moves within a narrow band between 10 and 18 percent.

## Conclusion

None of this means, of course, that socio-economic factors and economic causes are irrelevant to AfD voting. But a large body of research suggests that income, or objective material deprivation, plays a smaller role (for example <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11577-017-0446-1">Lengfeld 2017</a>). The data shown here confirm this picture: the AfD is not a party of low earners. Its support is similar across most of the income distribution and only falls among respondents with very high incomes. It is far more closely linked to formal education.

  /* ---------- 5) second vote by income, all parties ---------- */
  (function () {
    var stapel = ["CDU/CSU", "SPD", "Greens", "FDP", "Left", "AfD", "BSW", "Other"];
    var traces = stapel.map(function (p) {
      var v = D.vote_by_income[p];
      return { type: "bar", name: p, x: E, y: v, marker: { color: farben[p], line: { color: "white", width: 1 } },
        text: v.map(function (x) { return x >= 7 ? String(Math.round(x)) : ""; }), textposition: "inside", insidetextanchor: "middle",
        textfont: { size: 10.5, color: p === "FDP" || p === "Other" ? ink : "white" },
        customdata: v.map(function (x) { return f1(x); }),
        hovertemplate: "<b>%{x}</b><br>" + p + ": %{customdata}%<extra></extra>" };
    });
    Plotly.newPlot("afdinc-parties", traces, Object.assign({}, basis, {
      barmode: "stack", bargap: 0.25,
      xaxis: xInc, yaxis: pAxis(100, 25),
      legend: { orientation: "h", x: 0, y: 1.02, xanchor: "left", yanchor: "bottom", font: { size: 12 }, traceorder: "normal" },
      margin: { l: 50, r: 15, t: schmal ? 70 : 40, b: schmal ? 90 : 50 }
    }), cfg);
  })();

  /* ---------- 6) income distribution within each electorate ---------- */
  (function () {
    var blau = ["#e3eef7", "#c6dbef", "#9ecae1", "#6baed6", "#4292c6", "#2171b5", "#08519c", "#08306b"];
    var P = D.parties;
    var traces = E.map(function (e, j) {
      return { type: "bar", name: e, x: P, y: P.map(function (p) { return D.income_by_party[p][j]; }),
        marker: { color: blau[j], line: { color: "white", width: 1 } },
        customdata: P.map(function (p) { return [f1(D.income_by_party[p][j]), tsd(D.n_party[p])]; }),
        hovertemplate: "<b>%{x}</b><br>" + e + ": %{customdata[0]}%<br><span style='font-size:11px'>n = %{customdata[1]}</span><extra></extra>" };
    });
    Plotly.newPlot("afdinc-voters", traces, Object.assign({}, basis, {
      barmode: "stack", bargap: 0.25,
      xaxis: { fixedrange: true, tickfont: { size: schmal ? 10 : 12 }, tickangle: schmal ? -45 : 0 }, yaxis: pAxis(100, 25),
      legend: { orientation: "h", x: 0, y: 1.02, xanchor: "left", yanchor: "bottom", font: { size: 11.5 }, traceorder: "normal" },
      margin: { l: 50, r: 15, t: schmal ? 110 : 70, b: schmal ? 70 : 40 }
    }), cfg);
  })();

  /* ---------- 7) predicted share: AfD vs. Left ---------- */
  (function () {
    var traces = band(E, D.afd_left.AfD, farben.AfD, null, "AfD", true, "<b>AfD</b> · %{x}<br>")
      .concat(band(E, D.afd_left.Left, farben.Left, null, "Left", true, "<b>Left</b> · %{x}<br>"));
    Plotly.newPlot("afdinc-left", traces, Object.assign({}, basis, {
      xaxis: xInc, yaxis: pAxis(25, 5),
      legend: { orientation: "h", x: 0, y: 1.02, xanchor: "left", yanchor: "bottom", font: { size: 12 } },
      margin: { l: 50, r: 15, t: 40, b: schmal ? 90 : 50 }
    }), cfg);
  })();

  /* ---------- 8) low income vs. comparison groups, by party ---------- */
  (function () {
    var P = ["Left", "Greens", "SPD", "BSW", "CDU/CSU", "AfD", "Other"], L = D.contrast_labels;
    var sp = schmal ? 1 : 4, axes = gridAxes(P.length), traces = [], annos = [], shapes = [], lay = {};
    var rows = L.map(function (l, i) { return -i; });
    hoehe("afdinc-contrast-parties", 1700);
    P.forEach(function (p, k) {
      var c = D.contrast_parties[p], ax = axes[k];
      traces.push({ type: "scatter", mode: "markers", xaxis: ax.x, yaxis: ax.y, x: c.est, y: rows, showlegend: false,
        marker: { size: 10, color: "white", line: { color: farben[p], width: 2.5 } },
        error_x: { type: "data", symmetric: false, array: c.hi.map(function (h, i) { return h - c.est[i]; }), arrayminus: c.lo.map(function (l, i) { return c.est[i] - l; }), color: farben[p], thickness: 2, width: 4 },
        customdata: c.est.map(function (v, i) { return [vz(v), vz(c.lo[i]), vz(c.hi[i]), L[i]]; }),
        hovertemplate: "<b>" + p + "</b> · %{customdata[3]}<br>%{customdata[0]} pp<br><span style='font-size:11px'>95% CI: %{customdata[1]} to %{customdata[2]}</span><extra></extra>" });
      shapes.push({ type: "line", xref: ax.x, yref: ax.y + " domain", x0: 0, x1: 0, y0: 0, y1: 1, line: { color: ink3, width: 1, dash: "dot" } });
      annos.push(strip(ax, p));
      lay[ax.xaxis] = ppAxis(-30, 20, 10, { tickfont: { size: 9.5, color: ink2 } });
      lay[ax.yaxis] = { tickvals: rows, ticktext: L.map(function (l) { return wrap(l, 18); }), range: [-L.length + 0.4, 0.6], showgrid: false, zeroline: false, fixedrange: true,
                        showticklabels: k % sp === 0, tickfont: { size: 10.5 }, automargin: true };
    });
    Plotly.newPlot("afdinc-contrast-parties", traces, Object.assign({}, basis, lay, {
      annotations: annos, shapes: shapes,
      grid: { rows: Math.ceil(P.length / sp), columns: sp, pattern: "independent", roworder: "top to bottom", xgap: 0.06, ygap: schmal ? 0.18 : 0.3 },
      margin: { l: 10, r: 15, t: 40, b: 40 }
    }), cfg);
  })();

  /* ---------- 9) education vs. income from one joint model ---------- */
  (function () {
    var axes = gridAxes(2), traces = [], annos = [], lay = {};
    var B = D.education, S = D.slopes;
    var teile = [["School education", B, S.education], ["Monthly net household income", E, S.income]];
    hoehe("afdinc-slopes", 860);
    teile.forEach(function (t, k) {
      var ax = axes[k];
      traces = traces.concat(band(t[1], t[2], farben.AfD, ax, "AfD", false, "%{x}<br>"));
      traces.push({ type: "scatter", mode: "text", x: t[1], y: t[2].hi, xaxis: ax.x, yaxis: ax.y, showlegend: false, hoverinfo: "skip",
        text: t[2].est.map(function (v) { return f1(v, 0) + "%"; }), textposition: "top center", textfont: { size: 11, color: ink } });
      annos.push(strip(ax, t[0]));
      lay[ax.xaxis] = { fixedrange: true, tickangle: -40, tickfont: { size: 10.5 }, showgrid: false };
      lay[ax.yaxis] = pAxis(60, 10, { showticklabels: schmal || k === 0 });
    });
    lay.xaxis.domain = schmal ? [0, 1] : [0, 0.42];
    lay.xaxis2.domain = schmal ? [0, 1] : [0.48, 1];
    Plotly.newPlot("afdinc-slopes", traces, Object.assign({}, basis, lay, {
      annotations: annos,
      grid: { rows: schmal ? 2 : 1, columns: schmal ? 1 : 2, pattern: "independent", roworder: "top to bottom", xgap: 0.06, ygap: 0.4 },
      margin: { l: 50, r: 15, t: 40, b: 130 }
    }), cfg);
  })();
});
